# Reading the size off your tyre

Every bicycle tyre carries an ISO (ETRTO) marking. It is the only size
description that is never ambiguous, and it is the one this calculator wants.

## What to look for

Somewhere on the sidewall, in small print, you will find two numbers separated
by a dash:

```
28-622        or        622-28
```

- **622** is the bead seat diameter — the rim size. This is the ISO diameter.
- **28** is the tyre's nominal width in millimetres.

The larger number is always the diameter. They are sometimes printed in either
order, which is harmless once you know that.

## The nicknames, and why they mislead

The same sidewall usually also carries a traditional name:

| Printed | ISO | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 700 × 28c | 622-28 | Road and gravel |
| 29 × 2.1 | 622-53 | Same rim as 700c, much bigger tyre |
| 27.5 × 2.25 | 584-57 | Also sold as 650b |
| 26 × 1.95 | 559-50 | The MTB 26 inch |
| 27 × 1¼ | 630-32 | **Larger** than 700c |
| 20 × 1.75 | 406-44 | BMX and folders |
| 20 × 1⅛ | 451-28 | A *different* 20 inch |

Two things to take from that table:

1. **27 inch is bigger than 700c.** 630 mm against 622 mm.
2. **There are several unrelated 20 inch wheels**, and several 26 inch ones.
   Buying by the inch name alone is how people end up with a tyre that will not
   fit their rim.

## If the sidewall is unreadable

Measure instead:

- **Diameter:** deflate the tyre, measure across the rim from bead seat to bead
  seat (where the tyre's edge sits), through the centre. Round to the nearest
  common ISO size — 622, 584, 559, 507, 451, 406, 355, 305, 203.
- **Width:** inflate to your normal pressure and measure across the widest part
  of the tyre with calipers.

Measure the width rather than trusting the print. A tyre marked 25 mm sits
anywhere from 24 to 28 mm depending on how wide the rim under it is, and on a
modern wide rim the difference is enough to matter.

## Then skip all of it

If you want the number to be exactly right, do a roll-out instead. It accounts
for your weight, your pressure and your tyre's real shape in one measurement,
and none of the above can. The calculator has a helper for it.
